Video: The Importance of Small Business

1. How many employees can a business have to be considered a small business?

Fewer than 500 employees.

2. What percentage of the American job market is made up of small businesses? What percentage of US sales are those small businesses accountable for?

Small businesses provide about 55% of all jobs in the US and account for about 54% of all US sales.

3. Since 1990, big businesses have eliminated 4 million jobs and small businesses have added 8 million jobs. What is at least one reason big businesses would be eliminating jobs while small businesses are adding them?

Big businesses try to cut costs to increase profits, so they replace workers with technology and automation, move jobs to other countries where labor is cheaper, or cut positions after mergers. Small businesses, on the other hand, are new and growing, so every time a new business opens or expands it needs to hire people, and many of them (restaurants, shops, services) depend on human work that can't easily be automated.

Article: 4 Types of Entrepreneurship: Which One is Right for You

1. Choose a small business in your area and describe the product or service it provides.

A family-owned bakery (padaria) near my house. It sells fresh bread every morning, cakes, coffee, cheese bread and snacks, and people from the neighborhood stop there on their way to work or school. It is a typical small business: local, run by the owners, with no plan to become a chain.

2. According to the article, what is something that can allow scalable startups to grow faster than other new businesses?

Gaining investors. Scalable startups usually start small (in a garage or dorm room), but they attract investors, and that money allows them to grow and scale up quickly.

3. Richard Montañez … Explain how this is an example of intrapreneurship.

Intrapreneurship is when an employee acts like an entrepreneur inside the company they already work for. Montañez didn't start his own business: he was a custodian at Frito-Lay, saw an opportunity for a new product, and used the company's resources to bring it to life. Frito-Lay backed the idea, and he grew inside the company, becoming a vice president.

4. How does social entrepreneurship differ from traditional business entrepreneurship?

In traditional entrepreneurship, the main goal is to make a profit. In social entrepreneurship, the main goal is to create positive change and help people, not to make money. It is often done through a non-profit or an initiative that focuses on issues like the environment, racial justice or underserved communities.

1. From the list of potential business ideas you brainstormed above, select what you consider to be the best business idea and explain why.

My best business idea is the study café near my neighborhood. First, it solves a problem I live every day: there is no good place to study close to home, so I'm passionate about it. Second, there is real demand: many students in my area are preparing for entrance exams and the ENEM and have the same problem.
