Background

1. What is an ion? What is a valence electron? How is an ion formed?

An ion is an atom (or group of atoms) that has an electric charge because its number of electrons is different from its number of protons. A valence electron is an electron in the outermost energy level (shell) of an atom; these are the electrons involved in bonding and reactions. An ion is formed when an atom loses or gains valence electrons. If it loses electrons it becomes a positive ion (cation), and if it gains electrons it becomes a negative ion (anion). The number of protons never changes.

2. What do you think ionization energy means?

Ionization energy is the energy needed to remove an electron (usually the outermost, valence electron) from an atom, turning it into a positive ion. The first ionization energy is the energy needed to remove the first electron. The stronger the nucleus holds on to its valence electrons, the higher the ionization energy.

f. Make a comparison statement about the two elements in terms of why they require different amounts of ionization energy.

Krypton needs about 3 times more energy (1350 vs 418.5 kJ/mol) to lose an electron than potassium. Both are in period 4, so their valence electrons are in the same energy level, but krypton has 36 protons while potassium has only 19. The stronger positive charge of krypton's nucleus pulls the outer electrons closer (atomic radius 88 pm vs 243 pm) and holds them more tightly. Krypton also has a full outer shell (8 valence electrons), which is very stable, so it does not "want" to lose an electron. Potassium has only 1 valence electron, far from the nucleus and shielded by 18 inner electrons; losing it gives potassium the same stable electron arrangement as argon, so it comes off easily.

g. What trend in ionization energy do you observe for elements in the same period based on the data in the graph?

Ionization energy increases from left to right across a period. Each period starts at a low point with an alkali metal (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs) and rises to a peak at the noble gas at the end (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn). For example, in period 4 it goes from 418.5 kJ/mol (K) up to 1350 kJ/mol (Kr). There are a few small dips along the way (for example from Be to B and from N to O), but the overall trend is upward.

h. Make a prediction about the trend in ionization energy between atoms in the same group. Do atoms with larger atomic numbers have greater ionization energy than atoms with small atomic numbers in the same group?

No. I predict that ionization energy decreases down a group, so atoms with larger atomic numbers have lower ionization energy than atoms with smaller atomic numbers in the same group. On the graph, the peaks (noble gases) get lower each period, and the low points (alkali metals) also get lower. This makes sense because atoms further down a group have more energy levels, so their valence electrons are farther from the nucleus and more shielded by inner electrons, which makes them easier to remove.

3. Atomic radius and ionization energy

Which statement best describes these trends?

"Atoms that have small atomic radii will have large values of ionization energy."

Explain your choice referencing both the atomic model and subatomic particles:

In the atomic model, the positively charged protons are in the nucleus and the negatively charged electrons are in energy levels (shells) around it. Opposite charges attract, and the attraction is stronger when the electron is closer to the nucleus. In a small atom, the valence electrons are close to the nucleus, so the protons pull on them strongly and it takes a lot of energy to remove one. Across a period, each element has one more proton but the electrons stay in the same energy level, so the stronger nucleus pulls the shells in: the atom gets smaller and the ionization energy gets larger. In a large atom, the valence electrons are in an energy level far from the nucleus, and the inner electrons shield them from the pull of the protons, so they are held weakly and are easy to remove. Down a group, every element adds a new energy level, so the atoms get bigger and the ionization energy gets smaller.

The simulation shows this: helium is the smallest atom (31 pm) and has the highest ionization energy (2361.3 kJ/mol), while cesium is the largest of the ones I checked (298 pm) and has the lowest (375.5 kJ/mol). Krypton (88 pm, 1350 kJ/mol) compared to potassium (243 pm, 418.5 kJ/mol) shows the same pattern. So small atomic radius goes together with large ionization energy.
